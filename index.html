My approach

Most workflows aren't designed. They accumulate.

A problem appears, someone builds a fix, and the team moves on. Over time the fixes stack on top of each other. Feedback loops get longer, assets and costs get harder to track, and things get lost in the shuffle. In larger companies the effect compounds, because each department's patchwork has to coordinate with everyone else's.

I call these productivity off-ramps: every place the system pulls a team off the road to its goal. What's usually missing isn't a better tool. It's a holistic design.

That's why I don't start with a platform. I start with how the work moves, and choose the tools that serve that design, not the other way around.

I learned this in television post-production, but the pattern is the same anywhere work moves between people and systems.

Design it like an ecosystem

Ecosystems are the largest, most successfully engineered systems in the world. So I pattern my work after nature, and follow the work, the data, or the product through its whole life.

Four principles

01

Connect, don't silo.

Information should move between teams on its own, not wait for someone to chase it.

02

Automate the redundant.

If a task repeats, the system should do it, so people can spend their time on judgment.

03

Cut the noise.

Organize data into clear, digestible pieces. Show people what they need, when they need it.

04

Build for growth.

A good system has room for what comes next: new teams, new tools, and AI.

Track record

Putting it into practice at Paramount.

The situation

I ran the team at Paramount responsible for receiving and formatting acquired programming. When the software we used to track our work and expenses was being retired, it was up to me to decide what came next.

The decision

I chose not to recreate the old tool. I mapped the work through its whole life first, then ran a feasibility study on the platforms available. For that team, at that time, Airtable was the right fit, so I designed and built a new production-management system on Airtable around the four principles.

For another team, the right answer might be a different platform. The approach is what carries over.

Connected
Linked to other teams' systems, with real-time status and request notifications pushed to team messaging
Automated
Automatic record creation, rippling status updates, and built-in data validation
Focused
Context-sensitive interfaces and mobile dashboards showing each person only what's relevant
